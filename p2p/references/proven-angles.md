# P2P Proven Angles

An angle is the idea a piece of copy leads with: the hook for an email, an ad, or the opening of a sales video. Because most readers are won or lost in the first lines (see `copy-formulas.md`, section 3), the choice of angle matters more than anything written after it.

This file holds angle templates that have been tested repeatedly, the principles that make them work, and the procedure for filling them in.

## Why Use Proven Angles

- An original idea is an unknown variable. The only way to learn whether it works is to test it, and testing costs time and money. A proven template lets you get close on the first attempt.
- These templates work because they speak to the reader's shame, fear, and frustration. A reader addressed at that level feels understood, and a reader who feels understood assumes the writer must also have the solution. That is the strongest position of influence a writer can hold.

## The 8 Angle Templates

Examples use a weight-loss audience. Adapt the wording to the niche; keep the logic.

| # | Template | Why it works | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **This thing you're experiencing means your fear is coming true** | Ties a visible, everyday symptom to a feared outcome. Also moves blame onto a cause. | "Still hungry after meals? It may be a sign your metabolism is working against you." |
| 2 | **What you suspect is happening is happening** | Confirms a suspicion. The reader looks up to whoever confirms it and wants to hear more. | "Those clothes that stopped fitting? Your weight is affecting more than your mood, just as you feared." |
| 3 | **You're right about X, and that's the problem** | Agrees first (inside the bubble), then flips. | "You've always believed strict dieting is the way to lose weight. That belief may be what's holding you back." |
| 4 | **You can fix this without anyone having to know** | For problems that carry shame. Removes the fear of exposure. | "Start from home. No gym crowd, no public weigh-ins." |
| 5 | **Facing this problem? Here's what to do about it** (also: "If you have X, then do Y") | Simple and direct; calls out the problem and promises a next step. | "Struggling to lose weight after 35? Here's what to do." |
| 6 | **The logical solution only makes it worse** | Contrarian flip of the obvious fix. | "Think eating less will make you lose weight? Think again." |
| 7 | **It's not your fault. Do this to fix it** | Lifts the pressure off the reader, builds rapport and desire. | "If the weight keeps coming back, it isn't a willpower problem. It's what you were taught." |
| 8 | **Why the conventional method doesn't work, and what to do instead** | Contrarian plus a promised alternative. | "Why counting calories fails for most women over 35, and what works instead." |

Notes on use:

- **Template 1** is the most aggressive. The gentler form is often better: "the fact that you're experiencing [symptom] is a sign you might have [underlying cause]". This form also tells the reader the failure has a cause other than them. Skill guardrail: the link between symptom and outcome must be real and supportable. Do not invent medical, financial, or legal threats to frighten people.
- **Template 7** can be stated ("it's not your fault") or implied by naming an outside pressure (what the industry teaches, what society expects, a busy life). Implying works at least as well: the reader reaches "so it isn't on me" on their own.
- **Templates 3, 6, and 8** lead naturally into the same body: the commonly recommended fix is the cause, and the signature solution is the different approach.
- Templates combine. "You're right about X" plus "it's not your fault" is a common pairing.

## The 5 Principles Behind Strong Angles

Use these to judge any angle, including ones that fit no template. An angle wrapped around one or more of them has a high chance of working.

1. **Confirm their suspicions.** Tell them that what they suspected, or hoped was true, is true.
2. **Take a contrarian approach.** Hold a different belief from the rest of the market. A message that sounds like everyone else's is ignored; a different one gets heard. Most strong angles in practice are contrarian.
3. **Make it news.** "New research shows..." People want to know the latest discovery, and a research or scientific source adds credibility. Especially strong in health niches. Only use real, citable findings; never fabricate a study.
4. **Best-kept secret, hidden in plain sight.** Present the idea as something they should know about but have missed: an opportunity or explanation that was there all along.
5. **Emotional story.** A story that carries the feeling of the problem. Strongest when paired with a confirmed suspicion or a contrarian point.

Principles 3 and 4 combined ("new research points to a little-known reason...") are considered the most impactful type of angle. Example: new research suggesting the body has a weight "set point", presented as the missing piece the reader was never told about.

## Procedure: Generating Angles for a Project

1. Start from the research: problems at all three levels, fears, suspicions, failed solutions, dualities (`research-framework.md`).
2. For each template, fill the blanks with a specific item from the research. Skip a template if the research has nothing true to put in it (for example, template 4 when the problem carries no shame).
3. Produce at least one candidate per usable template, then add any news-related or hidden-in-plain-sight angle the research supports.
4. Tighten each candidate by hand: make the symptom concrete, cut soft wording, check it against the five principles. First-pass output is a starting point, not the final angle.
5. Pick the angle for the piece and carry it into the Verify step of the 6-Step Formula. Save the rest; each unused angle is another email or ad.

### Prompt skeleton (when drafting with an AI assistant)

Run it in the same conversation that holds the audience research so the model can reference it:

```
Use the problems and topics we uncovered in previous messages to
create angles using the following templates:
[paste the 8 templates]
```

How to treat the output:

- Expect uneven quality. Some lines will be strong, some generic, some will drift from the template. A drifted line can still be a good angle; judge it on the five principles.
- AI is best at surfacing problems and raw ideas. Take the ideas and write the final angle yourself; a writer who knows the audience usually produces a sharper line.
- Improve weak results with back-and-forth feedback, but do not lean on the tool as a crutch.

## Quick Check Before Committing to an Angle

- Does it start from something the reader has experienced or already believes?
- Does it touch shame, fear, or frustration, not just a surface inconvenience?
- Is it contrarian, a confirmed suspicion, news, a hidden secret, or an emotional story? (At least one.)
- Does it take blame off the reader, or at least not add to it?
- Is every factual claim in it true and supportable?
- Can the rest of the piece pay it off (a duality to pivot to, a mechanism to explain it)?
