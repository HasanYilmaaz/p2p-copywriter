# P2P Email Campaign Blueprints

Email-by-email outlines for every sequence in the P2P Email Machine. Read `email-machine.md` first for the architecture and the reasons behind it. Write each individual email with the 6-step formula in `copy-formulas.md` and the angle templates in `proven-angles.md`; build the automation with `email-automation-setup.md`.

How to use: pick the blueprint, fill in the slots for the user's offer, keep the order and the hand-offs between emails. Timing and counts marked "flexible" can be adjusted; the gating logic cannot.

Illustrative lines below are short paraphrased examples, not copy to reuse verbatim.

---

## A. Relationship Building Campaign (5-7 emails, one per day)

**Trigger:** subscriber joins (any entry point). **Goal:** relationship, authority, beliefs that make the offer the next logical step. **No selling.**

### Planning sheet (fill in before writing)

| Slot | Question |
|---|---|
| Entry frame | What did the opt-in page promise? What did the thank-you page say would arrive? |
| Theme | The one idea of the series, e.g. "the N lessons behind [result], one per day" |
| Length | N emails over N days (5-7) and the end point (event date, or "by the end you will...") |
| Character arc | Where the expert started, what changed internally, where they are now |
| Core duality | The belief shift the offer depends on (X is what stops you, not what helps you) |
| Main loop | The question asked in email 1 and answered in email 3 |
| Decision seed | The decision the expert made, mirroring the decision the reader will face |
| Objections | The 2-3 objections the later offer will meet; one story per objection |
| Micro-commitment links | Low-risk links for the PS (old videos, behind-the-scenes) |
| Pending action | What they signed up for but may not have done yet (join group, consume lead magnet) |

### Episode outline

**Email 1 -- Welcome and theme (sent immediately)**
Subject pattern: the name of the thing they joined + "is coming" (the same subject line shown on the confirmation page).
1. Greet; congratulate them on the step they just took and affirm it was a good decision
2. Promise: this will matter for them at whatever stage they are
3. Set expectations: what happens over the next days; repeat key dates
4. Introduce the theme as an idea that occurred to the expert: "what if I broke the principles down into N lessons and sent you one a day so you get the most out of [event/product]?" Use the first name here.
5. Raise curiosity about the tone: they will get the good, the bad, and the ugly
6. **Open the loop:** one question ("Do you know the number one thing that stops [people like you] from [outcome]?"), invite them to guess, promise the answer tomorrow
7. Sign-off with a slice of life (what the expert is heading to next)
8. PS: low-commitment link (e.g. the expert's earliest video -- "successful people were once where you are")
9. PPS: remind them of the pending action

**Email 2 -- The warning (day 2)**
Subject pattern: "[First name], have you ever been [negative experience]?"
1. Verify: a universal experience in this market (being lied to, burned, misled)
2. Validate the feeling it leaves (e.g. it makes you feel stupid)
3. Credibility by experience: the expert has seen the bad actors and fell for some of it too
4. Remove blame: as a beginner it is hard to know whom to trust
5. Care before content: "before I share anything, here is what to watch out for" -- 3 concrete warnings, each with its reason
6. Contrarian pledge: "here is what I will never do" -- 3 commitments mirroring the warnings
7. Repel: "if you are looking for [the quick-fix thing], this is not for you; you may as well unsubscribe", plus the reasons (long-term over short-term, it harms the industry, it does not work)
8. **Keep the loop open:** "I ran out of room for yesterday's answer -- tomorrow"
9. Slice-of-life sign-off

**Email 3 -- The answer: duality and decision (day 3)**
Subject pattern: "The [secret] answer I owe you"
1. Open mid-scene at the moment of highest tension: an exact time, a place, no preamble
2. Describe the scene in sensory detail so the reader is standing there
3. Show the life the reader does not want, observed in other people (hard-working, capable, still stuck)
4. State the expert's emotional reaction; the reader agrees ("me neither") and now shares the expert's view
5. Close the loop: what all those people had in common is not lack of intelligence, effort, or ability -- it is [the duality], e.g. following the conventional path and expecting unconventional results
6. The decision: "that day I decided to [renounce X]. It was the first day of the rest of my life."
7. Bridge to the offer moment without selling: "I will show you exactly how at [event/date]; hopefully that will be your day too"
8. Announce the remaining lessons; tease tomorrow's

**Emails 4 to N-1 -- One objection per episode**
For each: open on a tense line of dialogue or scene from the expert's story --> tell the story --> draw the lesson that dissolves one objection the offer will meet. Example: opening with a parent's disappointed reaction to the expert's risky choice, leading to "you do not need to see the whole path; put one foot in front of the other and it becomes clear". Each ends with a loop or a tease for the next episode.

**Final email -- Close the series**
The course example is unfinished past email 4, so this is an inference from the building blocks: recap the arc and the lessons, state the result the reader now has, close the remaining loops, and point at what happens next (the event, or simply that regular emails continue). If an Auto-Launcher follows, its first invitation can be sent next.

### Checks
- Is the theme announced in email 1 and the length stated?
- Is at least one loop open at the end of every email except the last?
- Does the arc show internal change, not just a list of achievements?
- Could a reader tell which offer is coming? They should not; they should only hold the beliefs that make it logical.

## B. Weekly Emails

**Value email (default).** Verify an experience --> validate the feeling --> reveal the deeper cause (the duality) --> short explanation --> what this changes for them. No offer. Optionally split into part 1 / part 2 across two sends with a cliffhanger.

**Hook email (occasional).** See invitation emails below. No click --> nothing else happens.

Sample two-week rhythm when running a Four-Day Cash Machine (flexible):

```
Wk 1: value | value (part 2) | INVITE 1
Wk 2: INVITE 2 | value | INVITE 3
Then: value-only weeks before the next hook
```

## C. Invitation Emails (the click sequence, Layer 2)

Used by all four campaigns: usually 3 emails, same structure, different angle each time. Sent as regular broadcasts; **each one excludes everyone who clicked a previous one.**

The invitation never mentions a product, price, or offer. It offers **free content** and asks for a click as a request to receive it.

Two framings from the course:

**1. "I just recorded a free training" (for a VSL).** Package the VSL as a how-to class: "a free training on how to [desired outcome] without [pain point]". Build the email with the 6-step formula or an angle template; the CTA is "click here and I'll send it to you".

**2. "Should I make this available again?" (for a video series).**
1. Casual opener with a small confession and a loop ("I don't really enjoy reading my comments -- I'll tell you why in a minute")
2. Create the need: an observation that the audience has been missing a certain kind of content
3. Rediscovery: the expert found a series they made earlier that got their best feedback and best viewer results
4. Gap: only a small fraction of the current audience ever saw it
5. Proposal: "what if I made it public once more, for a few days?"
6. Condition: "if enough of you want it, I will" -- a threshold number
7. CTA link worded as the reader's own reply: "Yes, make the series available again"
8. Promise to report back in 2-3 days
9. PS: postpone the opener's loop to the next email + slice of life

Only use claims that are true for the user's business (prior feedback, audience numbers, a threshold). If they are not available, keep the structure (need --> rediscovery --> proposal --> click to request) and drop the unsupported claims.

Invitations 2 and 3: same request, new angle, and close the loop left open in the previous one.

## D. Four-Day Cash Machine

**Use when:** there is a list and an existing VSL. Best first campaign for a new client.
**Assets:** VSL page with a 4-day timer; checkout that tags buyers; optionally a limited bonus.

```
Weekly stream:  ... value | INVITE 1 | INVITE 2 | (INVITE 3) | value | INVITE again ...   (~2 weeks)
                              \ click on any invite = enters automation, gets no more invites
Automation:
  +1 day    DELIVERY email: "here is the training" --> link to VSL
            (watch/click is tracked; wait up to ~2 days)
  no click on VSL --> exit, back to weekly emails (no countdown)
  clicked VSL, +1 day:
  CD 1   4 days left   -- restate the offer and what is included
  CD 2   3 days left   -- answer the main objection
  CD 3   2 days left   -- questions? reply / book a call / talk to the team
  CD 4   last day      -- closes today
  purchase at any point --> exit immediately
  end --> back to weekly emails
```

Notes:
- The VSL teaches toward an obvious conclusion: verify --> validate --> pivot to the philosophical problem --> explanation --> solution --> product. Length does not change the structure. (See `longform-canvas.md`.)
- The source describes the countdown both as four emails (4/3/2/last day) and as three (3 days / 2 days + objection / last day). Either works; match the number of emails to the real deadline.
- If the product is always available, the deadline belongs to the bonus: "get [main product] plus [bonus] only for the next four days".
- Hand the countdown segment to sales for personal outreach.

## E. Auto-Launcher (evergreen launch)

**Use when:** launch videos exist (3; 4 is possible, start with 3). Runs permanently.
**Typical placement:** triggered right after the Relationship Building Campaign; can also be entered from a weekly hook.

```
INVITE 1-3      (stop on first click)
  click:
V1 email        Video 1 -- THE PROBLEM
+1 day  V2      Video 2 -- THE SOLUTION
+1 day  V3      Video 3 -- THE PRODUCT as the vehicle for the solution; offer + sales page link
  clicks offer link on video 3 --> personal deadline starts (4 days, or 7; length is flexible)
CD 1  Offer is open -- recap for those who missed the last video: what it is, the bonus, the deadline
CD 2  Do you have questions? -- book a call here / message the team here
CD 3  Less than 24 hours -- closes today (largest sales spike)
  purchase --> exit; end --> back to weekly emails
```

**Video 1 delivery email outline:**
1. "You convinced me" -- report the result of the invitation (e.g. number of requests, screenshot if real)
2. Deliver as promised, with a catch: the next video unlocks only after 24 hours
3. Reason for the catch: the content only helps if applied; the gap is time to reflect and act
4. Preview what each of the three videos covers and when it arrives
5. Tell them the videos come by email, so watch the inbox
6. Open a loop answered inside the video ("Do you know what your current [X] is? Guess; the answer is in video 1")
7. Link + reason to watch now (available for 24 hours)

Videos 2 and 3 emails: short; close the previous loop, state what this video delivers, give the reason to watch today. A viewer who drops out before video 3 never sees an offer.

Deadlines are per person, counted from their own action, which is what lets the campaign run for every new subscriber with honest urgency. Availability windows on the videos must be enforced on the page if the email states them.

## F. One-Time Launch

**Use when:** a strong 3-video series and a meaningful list; once or twice a year (more often is possible, but producing the videos is heavy). Usually run across email and social channels at once.

```
INVITE 1-3   to the whole list (clickers = hot segment; they believe they requested it, so they engage more)
V1, V2, V3   sent to EVERYONE, clicked or not, one day apart
             offer appears only in video 3, with a link to the sales page
  clicked through to the sales page and did not buy --> COUNTDOWN (fixed cart-close date)
  did not click the sales page --> no further emails about it; weekly emails resume
```

Why everyone: gating by click shrinks the audience at each step; sending the videos to the full list gives several times more viewers for the same content. It costs little goodwill because the reader experiences five content emails and a single offer. Do not repeat this pattern often.

Rough click-through ranges quoted in the course for planning (a large list; yours will differ): invitation 1 and video 1 about 5-15%, second about 5-10%, third about 3-5%; about 1% of total clickers buying at a ~$1,000 price.

## G. Goodwill Campaign

**Use when:** you want revenue from the people who cannot afford the main offer, while increasing goodwill. Needs a low-ticket product, typically cut from the main one (its first module or the first lessons of each phase).

```
INVITE 1-3   (stop on first click)
  click:
V1   pure value: teach something useful, expect nothing
V2   pure value + soft pitch at the end (the pitch may also appear in both videos)
```

Soft pitch outline: "you may know I have [main product] at [high price]. If that is out of reach right now, I packaged its core principles into [small product] for [low price], so you can start getting a part of those results." Same video structure as always, but non-aggressive. Inside the low-ticket product, buyers are offered the upgrade to the main one.

No countdown is prescribed for this campaign. Afterwards: back to weekly emails.

## H. Subject Lines and Preheaders

The email module gives no separate subject-line formula; these patterns are taken from its worked examples:

| Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Announcement tied to what they joined | "The [event name] is coming" |
| Personal question with first name | "[Name], have you ever been lied to?" |
| Paying off a loop from the previous email | "The secret answer I owe you" |
| Curiosity through an odd confession | "Why I hate reading [my own comments]" |
| Plain status for countdowns | "Offer is open" / "Do you have any questions?" / "Less than 24 hours left" |

Guidance:
- Sound like a person writing to one person; no promotional styling
- In a serialized sequence, let the subject refer back to the open loop so the reader recognizes the next episode
- The first email's subject must be exactly what the confirmation page announced
- Countdown subjects are literal; clarity beats cleverness when the reader already knows the offer
- Always write the preheader instead of leaving the default; use it to extend the curiosity, not to repeat the subject
