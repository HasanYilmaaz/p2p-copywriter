# Turkish Headline Formulas (50 Most-Opened Patterns)

Fifty fill-in-the-blank headline patterns in Turkish, each with one example. The instructor compiled the list from an analysis of the most-clicked headlines online, so these are patterns that have already earned clicks, not invented ones.

## How to use this file

**Where they fit**

- YouTube video titles and thumbnail text
- Reels / Shorts / TikTok hooks (the first spoken sentence and the on-screen text)
- Email subject lines
- Landing page and opt-in page headlines
- Ad headlines and the first line of ad primary text

**How to fill the blanks**

1. Start from the audience, not the product. Have their desire (what they want to happen) and their obstacle (what they fear, hate doing, or keep failing at) in front of you. See `research-framework.md` for the internal / external split.
2. Put the desire in the "result" blank and the obstacle in the "without / even if / stop" blank. Formula 1 is the cleanest case: `Nasıl [engel] Yapmadan [arzu]ya Ulaşabilirsiniz?`
3. Make every blank specific: a number, a time frame, a platform, a named group. "30 günde 100 bin takipçi" beats "daha fazla takipçi".
4. Use the audience's own words for the problem. If they say "erteleme", do not write "zaman yönetimi eksikliği".
5. Fix the suffixes. The blanks carry Turkish case endings (-yı, -da, -dan, -nın); rewrite the sentence so it reads naturally after filling. A grammatically broken headline is worse than no formula.
6. Do not invent numbers, results or authority names. A figure in a headline must be true and deliverable by the content behind it.

**How to choose**

- Never deliver a single headline. Write 5 to 10 variants drawn from at least three different groups below.
- Score them against the 8 P2P headline criteria in `landing-page-rules.md` (self-interest, implied proof, hope, tension, something new, and so on) and recommend the top two or three with a one-line reason each.
- Rule of thumb from the instructor: there is no hard word limit, but roughly 5 to 10 words is the working range. The pattern matters more than the count.
- For paid ads, check the platform policy notes in `advertising-rules.md` before using money amounts, health claims or "you are X" call-outs. Several examples below would need softening on Meta.
- The headline is a promise. The content, page or email behind it has to pay it off.

Blanks are shown as `…`. Numbers are the original numbering of the sheet.

## Notes on the source sheet

- The examples lean toward business and online marketing because that was the instructor's audience. The patterns transfer to any niche; replace the examples, keep the shape.
- A few formulas and their examples differ slightly in wording on the original sheet (11, 26, 33). Both wordings are usable.
- Formula 40's example was garbled in the source and is reconstructed here as "Mehmet Öz'den …".
- The sheet shows formula 44 as "… kişisi Batarken NE YAPMALISIN?"; the blank is the person or group that is going under.
- Capitalised words (ÇOK GEÇ, NASIL DURDURURSUNUZ, UYARI) are part of the pattern: one stressed phrase per headline, not the whole line.
