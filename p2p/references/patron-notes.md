# Patron Notes: The Instructor's Working Method (Turkish Teaching)

Rules of thumb, formulas and annotated examples from the instructor's Turkish teaching: one podcast episode and one live Q&A on copywriting. Explanations are in English; formulas, key terms and example copy stay in Turkish.

Use this file as a lookup when writing in the "patron" voice or for a Turkish-speaking market. Where his teaching matches a P2P framework, the match is noted in one line and the framework itself is not repeated here.

Transcripts were auto-generated, so the example lines are cleaned reconstructions, not verbatim quotes. Platform figures (character limits, CTR benchmarks, emoji and engagement-bait rules) are what he reported at the time of recording; verify them before relying on them.

## 1. The one formula behind every piece

He says he uses the same formula in every text: emails, ads, sales pages, video scripts, seminar scripts.

| # | Adım | What goes here |
|---|------|----------------|
| 1 | **Arzular** (içsel + dışsal) | What the audience wants, outside and inside |
| 2 | **Problemler** (dışsal, içsel, felsefi) | What stands in the way |
| 3 | **Çözüm yöntemi / yol haritası** | The route out, as a method or a few steps |
| 4 | **Rehber** | Who or what guides them: a mentor, a research finding, a method, an expert presentation |
| 5 | **Cennet / cehennem** | What they gain if they act, what continues if they do not |
| 6 | **Call to action** | Often one CTA mid-way and one again at the end |

How he applies it:

- Not every piece carries all six. Short ads often skip cennet/cehennem; the guide may be only a pointer to the page.
- Price does not change the formula. For expensive offers the funnel type changes, not the copy formula.
- Sector does not change the formula. Asked about construction projects, his answer was that the buyer is still a person with desires and problems.
- Order of 3 and 4 flips by format: in ads he teases the solution then points to the guide; in the sales video the guide comes first and then lays out the roadmap.

P2P matches: steps 1–2 are the 3 Layers of Problems (`research-framework.md`). Cennet/cehennem is a duality (`core-principles.md`), the same contrast as Before–After. The whole sequence runs parallel to the 6-Step Formula (`copy-formulas.md`): desires and problems cover Verify and Validate, the guide and roadmap cover Explanation and Benefit, and both end on a CTA.

## 2. Research: knowing the reader

**List desires and problems before writing a word.** His worked example for a property developer's buyer:

- Dışsal arzular: parasına para katmak; en kârlı gayrimenkulü bulmak; kolay kiraya verebilmek, istediği an satabilmek; (yabancı alıcı için) oturum almak.
- İçsel arzular: kendini saygın hissetmek; saygın insanlarla komşu olmak; hastaneye yakın olup güvende hissetmek.
- Problemler: emlakçılara güvenmiyor; nereye yatırım yapacağını bilmiyor; doğru yatırım yaptığından emin olamıyor; kredi çıkaramıyor; evrak işlerini beceremiyor.

Then the copy almost writes itself: "Gayrimenkule yatırım yapmak isteyenlerin en büyük arzusu şu… Bu arzuyu yakından takip etmemize rağmen en büyük problemin de şu olduğunun farkındayız. Bu problemi çok iyi anlıyoruz. Çözümü için 35 yıllık bir gayrimenkul şirketi olarak…"

**Finding internal problems.** Stay in contact with the audience, talk to them, spend time with them, read the comments in the places where they spend time. There is no shortcut.

**The recognition test.** If you have understood the internal problem and use the right words, the reader says "Evet, benim problemim tam olarak bu." If they do not react that way, the analysis was wrong, not the reader. You cannot tell people you know their problem better than they do; they have to feel it. Same idea as P2P "understand the reader better than they understand themselves".

**Write to one person sitting next to you.** He names this as his own biggest edge: while writing he pictures the reader beside him. The common failure is writing as if you were the audience. "Güzel oldu" is not the test. The test is whether the reader will understand it and what they will think at each line.

**Empathy line by line.** Ask of every sentence: when I say this, what will they think, what will they feel, what question will it raise, and what must I say next to remove that question? He describes all ad and email copy as empathy applied this way.

**Picture the real listener, not the room.** For a webinar he reviewed, he described the actual viewer as someone sceptical, half expecting a scam, watching on a phone in a café, short of money and time, who gave it fifteen minutes. That person owes the speaker nothing. Colleagues and the boss are not the audience.

**Reverse-engineer winners ("hacking").** Find ads that have run for a long time in the ad library; longevity means the ad is working. Take them apart with four questions: **Ne yapmış? Neden yapmış? Nasıl yapmış? Ben nasıl yapabilirim?** Then rewrite the structure for a different topic. He notes his own best ad stayed in use for years because nothing written against it could beat it.

## 3. Hooks and headlines

**The first sentence must be an olta.** Body copy can be plain paragraphs; the opening line has to hit. He places the hook at the very top of the ad text, not only in the headline field.

**Marketing is attention.** His definition: marketing is the moment someone's eyes are elsewhere and you make them look. The only thing it has to produce is "enteresanmış, daha fazla bilgi almak istiyorum". Everything after the click is sales. So the hook, message and creative carry the marketing job.

Hook types he demonstrates:

| Type | Örnek | Why it works |
|------|-------|--------------|
| Fatigue question from the audience's history | "Emek ve zaman harcamaktan yoruldunuz mu?" | Written after analysing people who have dieted again and again and are worn out. P2P Verify step. |
| Reversal of what others say | "Boş işlerle uğraşma." | The line the people around him used on him; the copy then shows those "boş işler" changed his life. A built-in duality. |
| Throwing rocks at the market | "Herkes online ticaretten bahsediyor. Herkes 'şunu yapın, bunu yapın' diyor. Peki beni tanıyor musun? Bu işin bana uygun olduğunu nereden biliyorsun?" | Voices the reader's irritation at competitors' ads, then offers criteria to choose with. P2P "throw rocks at enemies". |
| Borrowed authority | Show a well-known author's book and open with how you met them and what they told you | Three gains at once: the book is the hook, the name lends authority, and the ad can be targeted at that person's followers. Use only if the connection is real. |
| Imagination | Claude Hopkins's toothpaste ad: "Dilinizi dişlerinizin üzerinde gezdirin. Bir tabaka hissedeceksiniz." | Makes the reader act out the problem and discover it. |
| Statistic tied to a desire | A research figure about what the audience cares about, placed before the problem | His proposed improvement to the Hopkins ad: it finds the problem but has no desire in it, so open with one. |
| Curiosity plus promise (video) | "Türkiye'nin en kârlı gayrimenkul yatırımlarından birini merak ediyorsanız, bu videoda sizi o yatırımla tanıştıracağım." | Names the desire and promises the reveal. |

**Statistics must be real.** In his own ad he left the number as X and told the team to find the actual study: "Yalan yanlış şeyler sallamayın, gerçek rakamı yazın." Do the same: leave a marked placeholder and ask the user for the source.

**Headline moulds.** He names: "[Yıl]'ın en büyük fırsatı"; result + time frame without the sacrifice ("6 ayda 12 kilo ver, sevdiğin besinlerden vazgeçmeden"); and the "Nasıl…" family (using X, without X, by date X). The full list is in `turkish-headline-formulas.md`. No strict word limit; about 5 to 10 words.

**Questions inside copy.** When a paragraph ends on a question, do not answer it. The job is to raise curiosity; the answer lives on the page the link goes to.

**On-screen text in short videos.** Asked why the text over his Reels is coloured, he said the styling is the designers' choice; the aim is simply that the hook catches the eye.

## 4. Writing ads

### The job of an ad

An ad does not sell. It makes the reader curious, gets the click and sends them to the page. The page or funnel does the converting. "Öyle bir metin yazacağım ki okuyan satın alacak" is the mistake. Matches the P2P rule to sell the click and to keep product for later.

### Do not describe the product

Asked about Hopkins's advice to explain every product detail, he disagreed: do not list the features of the thermos; say which problem it solves for the person who buys it, what need it grew out of and why it will help them. Matches P2P "lead with the problem, never sell the product first".

### Annotated short ad

A short ad he wrote in a few minutes for a clinic offering a medical weight-loss procedure:

| Satır | Role |
|-------|------|
| "Emek ve zaman harcamaktan yoruldunuz mu?" | Olta. Speaks to people exhausted by repeated diets. |
| "Daha güzel ve estetik görünmek bu kadar zor olmak zorunda değil." | Shows they are understood; names the desire. |
| "Yapılan araştırmalara göre ortalama bir insan yılda X kere diyete başlıyor. Kadınlarda bu sayı çok daha fazla :)" | Problem, verified with a real figure. The aside is deliberate: a light, playful touch to earn comments and shares. |
| "Peki size bu problemin 15 dakikalık bir çözümü olduğunu söylesem? Hem de servet ödemeden." | Solution teased, not explained. Raises "nasıl yani?". |
| "Aşağıdaki linke tıklayarak uzman doktorlarımızın hazırladığı ücretsiz sunumu izleyin ve yol haritanızı keşfedin." | Rehber + CTA. In a short ad the guide is only pointed to. |
| "Daha güzel, estetik ve sağlıklı günlere." | Sign-off. |

No cennet/cehennem here, and he points out that it works without it.

**Extending it with cennet/cehennem:**

- Cennet: "Sunumu izleyerek uzman doktorlarımızın üç adımlık formülünü keşfedecek, sevdiğiniz yemeklerden vazgeçmeden de sağlığınıza kavuşabileceğinizi göreceksiniz."
- Cehennem: "Bu reklamı da diğerleri gibi görmezden gelip kaydırmaya devam ederseniz, yılda defalarca diyete başlamaya; paranızı, vaktinizi ve ömrünüzü diyetlere ve spor salonlarına yatırmaya devam edeceksiniz."
- Then the CTA again.

Policy caution: this example makes health and personal-attribute claims that Meta restricts. Before reusing the shape for a paid ad, apply the policy rules in `advertising-rules.md`.

### Long paragraph ad

His longest-running ad is plain paragraphs under a three-word hook. It tells his own story through the audience's desires and problems, lands on the solution he found and ends on a CTA. The single message: everyone said stop wasting your time, and that very thing turned his life around. Bullet points are optional; most of his ads are paragraphs.

### Voice

Write the way the reader thinks, in their own inner voice:

> "Daha sağlıklı olmak bu kadar zor olmamalı. Ömrümü diyet yaparak geçirmek istemiyorum. Sevdiğim yemeklerden istediğim kadar yemek, ama sağlıklı da yaşamak istiyorum. Teknolojinin bu kadar geliştiği bir dönemde bunun hâlâ bir çözümü olmamasına inanamıyorum, diye düşünüyorsanız doğru yerdesiniz."

The reader should finish that paragraph thinking "ben de tam olarak böyleyim". Compare the corporate version he rejects: "Yaptığımız analizlere göre insanların yüzde X'inin şu sıkıntıları olduğunu tespit ettik. Ayrıntılı bilgi için linke tıklayın."

### Length and format

- Long versus short is not decided by product type. Test both with the same creative. He has winners of both kinds; in the education niche long copy wins for him.
- "Short" does not mean a design with "ayrıntılı bilgi için tıklayın" and nothing else. Minimum: one real paragraph, then the link.
- Video generally outperforms image for him. Test the same text with a video and with an image.
- For things that cannot be photographed yet (an unbuilt project), use video with a person speaking over the empty site, the model and the renders, so the viewer keeps listening while the visuals change.
- Instagram caption limit was 2,200 characters at the time. Look up the current limit rather than trusting a remembered number.
- Emojis make text easier to read, but his team stopped using many of them after ads were penalised for overuse. Asking for "beğen, yorum yap, paylaş" inside an ad was banned outright. Platform rules shift; check current policy.

### Retargeting ads

Two groups: visited but left no email; left an email but did not buy. For either, ask "neden?" and write the long list: did not understand the offer, did not trust me, did not trust the product, did not feel the need, and so on. Then:

1. Open with their desire.
2. Treat each reason as a problem.
3. Answer every reason while describing the guide and the roadmap.
4. Call to action again, from a different angle.
5. Skip cennet/cehennem; it is not needed again here.

**Dialogue format.** One of his retargeting ads is written as a conversation: a friend asks the doubts ("Herkes eğitim satıyor, senin farkın ne?", "Peki şöyle diyorlar, buna ne diyorsun?") and he answers each one. The friend speaks for everyone who did not sign up. The same device works in video.

**Aim retargeting at the next step only.** Someone who never left an email is asked to register, not to buy.

### Competitors

Throw rocks at how the market advertises, never at a named competitor's product. His example hook attacks the "arabamı aldım, evimi aldım, çünkü şunu yapıyorum" style of ad and offers the opposite: he will not tell you which business to pick, he will give you the criteria to pick for yourself. He uses this in seminars and in every kind of copy.

### Borrowed authority

Worth using when the authority is widely known and the connection is real. Citing a figure few people recognise adds nothing; in that case leave it out.

### Local businesses

Everyone nearby already knows the barber exists and still does not come. Awareness is not the problem. Give a reason to show up: a dated event or occasion at the location.

### Offer framing: check-up instead of sales meeting

Asking for a sales meeting raises the other side's guard at once; they see your eyes on their wallet. Offering to examine their situation, find the problem points and hand over a prescription or report reads as a favour, and people accept it. Useful for lead-generation CTAs.

### Writing for another language

Write it in your native language first. Shorten and sharpen it there. Translate. Then check grammar and word choice, and whether the rhythm and emphasis survived; ask someone fluent if you can. A foreign audience needs copy in its own language, not yours.

## 5. Writing emails

### What the email is for

- **Registered but has not engaged** (did not attend, did not view the presentation, did not look at the product, nothing in cart): do not push the sale. Push them to the page so they get to know the offer first.
- **Warm and stuck** (added to cart, attended and did not buy): sell inside the email. List the arguments that remove the doubts in their head and add social proof.
- After a non-purchase, a follow-up sequence that shows cennet and cehennem lifts sales. This is the teaching the Q&A opened from.

### Siz or sen

| Audience | Register | Reason |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Left an email, has not bought (cold, large list) | **Siz** | No relationship yet; familiarity can be misread. |
| Buyers, upsell and second-product offers | **Sen** | They know you; closeness is earned and reads as warmth. |

Set the register by how cold, lukewarm or hot the list is.

### Multiple voices

The same campaign can be sent in different voices and from different sender addresses: the founder, the team, support, the company.

Founder's email after an event:

> "Merhaba [isim], dün açtığımız seminere katıldığını ama eğitimi almadığını gördüm. Almama sebebin aşağıdakilerden biriyse çözümü şu: …"

Team resend about twelve hours later, subject **"[Kurucu] Bey'in mailini açtınız mı?"**:

> "Merhaba sevgili [isim], on iki saat önce [kurucu] Bey size bir mail göndermişti. Yoğunluktan göremediğinizi tahmin ettiğim için aşağıya tekrar bırakıyorum. Okumanızı tavsiye ederim; okursanız şunu, şunu elde edeceksiniz. Okumazsanız şunları kaçırabilirsiniz. Mailin orijinal hali aşağıda."

The resend is a small cennet/cehennem for the act of reading, in the team's voice and in "siz".

### Images

Images exist to raise curiosity and to break up long text. Use GIFs and screenshots of the important parts of the destination page. In warm sales emails, place social proof alongside them.

### Deliverability

Spam placement follows engagement. Write emails people open and react to; do not send boring ones; do not send the wrong message to the wrong segment; use interesting subject lines so open rates stay high.

### Lists you did not build

He advises against cold "tanışma" emails to bought or borrowed lists: the reaction is "sen kimsin, bana niye mail atıyorsun?" and spam complaints. Instead, load the list as a custom audience, run ads to it and email only those who opt in.

### Layout

Font choice hardly matters. Slightly larger type, readable, not cramped. He says he does not know which font his own emails use.

## 6. Writing sales videos

The landing-page sales video follows the master formula in this order:

1. **Arzular.** Open with what the audience wants, inside and outside. (Language-course example: millions want to learn the language for career and other reasons.)
2. **Problemler**, external, internal and philosophical. ("Kelime ve gramer ezberletildiği için yıllarca eğitim almamıza rağmen öğrenemiyoruz"; this turns into chaos until the person no longer wants to try.)
3. **Rehber.** A guide appears: research showing a different way of learning is far more effective, and the brand that applied it.
4. **Yol haritası.** "Bir, şunu yapın. İki, şunu. Üç, şunu." (See it, hear it, be exposed to it constantly, and do not get bored.)
5. **Call to action.** "Şimdi aşağıdaki linke tıklarsanız…"
6. **Başarı** (cennet). What life looks like after. His image: "Dünyaya anahtar deliğinden değil, kapıyı açarak bakmaya başlarsınız."
7. **Başarısızlık** (cehennem). What stays the same if they do nothing: limited information, missed opportunities.
8. **Call to action again.**

Step 2 uses the P2P 3 Layers of Problems as-is. For the long-form version of this structure see `longform-canvas.md`.

He frames the stakes this way: one script, written once, keeps earning for as long as it runs, which is why the sales-video script is the highest-leverage text in a business.

## 7. Seminar and webinar copy

- Same formula, same rock-throwing. The registration ad promises something concrete the seminar then delivers (in his example: the criteria for choosing your own online business).
- Give something tangible early. His stop-the-rehearsal test: twenty minutes in, has the listener heard anything they can hold on to? If not, they are gone.
- Earn the right to advise. To a stranger the speaker is nobody; address their situation and doubts before giving advice.
- Speak to the person who will actually attend (section 2), not to the room you rehearse in.

## 8. Editing

- **Write, then go back and cut.** State the message, write the draft, return and ask "Bu metni nasıl kısaltabilirim?" He admits he skips this for lack of time and says he should not.
- **More full stops than commas.** The more commas, the worse the copy. Long literary sentences belong in books.
- **Kısa ve net cümleler.** Slogan-like, clean, fast. The text should move "tık, tık, tık".
- **Check the punch.** After cutting: "Yeterince vurucu yazabildim mi?"
- **Sincere and a little fun.** Write without boring the reader; touch what they feel. Formal report language is the most common problem he sees in newly hired copywriters.
- **Paragraph flow.** He uses no special transition phrases between paragraphs: a blank line, a new paragraph, the next step of the formula. (P2P recommends a connecting-words library; in the patron voice, lean on white space and short sentences.)

Matches the P2P simplicity principle and the length filter in `persuasion-filters.md`.

## 9. Testing and measuring

- **How many variants?** No upper limit. At least four per audience.
- **Copy or audience at fault?** Cross-test four or five texts against four or five audiences. A text that fails across many audiences is the problem; discard it. Keep the best audience and the best creative, test new creatives against that audience, keep whichever wins, then try new audiences against the winner. Never stop.
- **No opinions.** "Bence bu kitle iyidir" and "uzun metin okunmaz" are not arguments. If a long ad is still running, the data chose it.
- **Test small things.** Same design with long and short text, landscape and portrait, colour variants, video and still, different cover images.
- **What counts as a good ad?** Click-through rate. His benchmarks at the time: e-commerce at least about 3%; education and similar niches around 1% is acceptable. Sector-dependent.
- **Longevity is the strongest signal.** A text the algorithm keeps preferring over every challenger is a winner.
- **Hit rate.** There is no known ratio of winners per ten texts.

## 10. Building the skill

- **It is a muscle.** Watching videos about push-ups does not produce push-ups. Write, write, write.
- **Write by hand, constantly.** Notes while reading, notes while listening. He credits a lifelong habit of note-taking for his fluency.
- **It is engineering, not talent.** He describes himself as a numbers person, not a words person: there is a structure in your head and you write to the structure. Matches P2P "never start from a blank page".
- **Blank-page freeze.** Normal for beginners. The cure is the formula plus repetition until it becomes reflex.
- **AI is an assistant.** Content can be produced with AI, but an ad that wins cannot be had by handing the job over. You must know the formula and direct the tool.
- **Being copied.** It cannot be prevented, and it means the work is good. The answer is to be so good and so fast that copies lag behind.
- **Reading he recommends:** Gary Provost, *100 Ways to Improve Your Writing*; David Ogilvy's ads and books; Robert Cialdini, *Influence* and his other books; Claude Hopkins. He re-reads a few books many times rather than reading many once.

## 11. Recurring questions, short answers

| Soru | His answer |
|------|------------|
| Başlıklar halinde mi, paragraf mı? | Either. Most of his ads are paragraphs. Only the hook has to be striking. |
| Başlık kaç kelime olmalı? | No limit; about 5–10. The mould matters more. |
| Uzun mu, kısa mı? | Test both. Education: long wins for him. |
| Pahalı ürün için ayrı formül var mı? | No. Same formula; the funnel changes. |
| Her sektör için ayrı yaklaşım? | No. Start from the buyer's desires and problems. |
| Ürünün tüm detaylarını anlatmalı mıyım? | No. Say which problem it solves and why it helps. |
| Her zaman cennet/cehennem gerekir mi? | No. Short ads and retargeting often go without it. |
| Sorunun cevabını metinde vermeli miyim? | No. Raise curiosity; the answer is on the page. |
| Rakibe taş atılır mı? | Yes, at the market's approach. Not at a competitor's product. |
| Başkasının otoritesini kullanabilir miyim? | Yes, if widely known and the link is real. |
| Sen mi, siz mi? | Cold list: siz. Buyers: sen. |
| Emoji? | Aids reading; platforms have penalised overuse. Check current rules. |
| Kaç metin test etmeliyim? | At least four; no upper limit. |
| Yabancı dilde reklam? | Write in your own language, shorten, translate, check the rhythm. |
| Paragraf geçişleri için özel kelime? | None. White space and the formula. |
| Yapay zeka ile tutan metin yazılır mı? | Only as an assistant to someone who knows the formula. |

## 12. Mistakes he warns about

1. Writing for yourself instead of for the reader.
2. Trying to make the sale inside the ad.
3. Listing product features instead of the problem solved.
4. Formal, report-style language.
5. Long sentences full of commas.
6. Not going back to shorten.
7. Inventing statistics.
8. A "short ad" that is only a visual and "tıklayın".
9. Deciding length, audience or format by opinion instead of testing.
10. Pushing a sale by email to people who have not yet seen the offer page.
11. Cold-emailing lists that never opted in.
12. Citing an authority nobody knows.
13. Twenty minutes of talk with nothing tangible for the listener.
14. Advising strangers before earning the right.
15. Confusing the funnel with marketing: marketing wins attention, the funnel sells.
16. Expecting AI to write a winner without knowing the formula yourself.
